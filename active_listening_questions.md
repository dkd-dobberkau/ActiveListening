# Active Listening — Question Catalogue

*Sharp questions for deeper conversations*

---

This catalogue is a practical companion for meetings, 1-on-1s, and conversations. The questions are designed to slow things down, create space, and draw out what really matters. Remember: the goal is not to ask all of them, but to have a few ready that fit the moment.

**Core principle: Ask one question. Then wait. Silence is data.**

---

## 1. Understanding the Core

These questions cut through surface-level discussion to find what actually matters. Use them when someone is explaining a situation, presenting a problem, or making a request.

| Question | What it does |
|----------|-------------|
| *Why is this important to you?* | Surfaces personal motivation and emotional stakes beyond the rational argument. |
| *What is the core issue here?* | Forces prioritization. Cuts through symptoms to root causes. |
| *If you could only solve one thing, what would it be?* | Reveals true priority when everything feels urgent. |
| *What would change if we did nothing?* | Tests urgency and clarifies real consequences vs. assumed ones. |
| *What are you most worried about?* | Opens the emotional dimension of a seemingly rational discussion. |
| *What does success look like for you here?* | Aligns expectations before jumping into solutions. |

---

## 2. Deepening and Mirroring

These help you stay in the listening mode. They signal to the other person that you are truly present and invite them to go further. Especially powerful after someone shares something meaningful.

| Question | What it does |
|----------|-------------|
| *What I'm hearing is [X]. Is that right?* | Mirrors back. Shows you listened. Lets them correct or go deeper. |
| *You said [X]. Tell me more about that.* | Opens a door they may have only cracked. No judgment, just curiosity. |
| *What do you mean by [specific word]?* | Catches assumptions. Words like "fair", "respect", "quality" mean different things to different people. |
| *Can you give me an example?* | Moves from abstract to concrete. Reveals what someone actually experienced. |
| *How did that land with you?* | Invites emotional response without forcing it. |
| *What's the part you haven't said yet?* | Creates permission for the unsaid. Use with trust and care. |

---

## 3. Creating Space

Not questions in the traditional sense, but moves that create room for others to think and speak. These are the Wu Wei of conversation: doing less to achieve more.

| Move | What it does |
|------|-------------|
| *[Silence — 5 seconds]* | Lets the other person process. Many people need a beat before they share something real. |
| *Take your time.* | Removes time pressure. Signals genuine interest. |
| *I'd like to understand this better.* | Positions you as learner, not judge. Disarms defensiveness. |
| *That sounds important. Say more.* | Validates without evaluating. Opens the door wider. |
| *I notice you paused there.* | Gently draws attention to a nonverbal cue. Use with warmth. |
| *Let me sit with that for a moment.* | Models reflective behavior. Shows that thinking before responding has value. |

---

## 4. Team Meetings and 1-on-1s

Questions tuned for the contexts you navigate most: leading your teams at dkd, individual check-ins with direct reports, and collaborative working sessions.

### Opening a meeting

| Question | What it does |
|----------|-------------|
| *What's the one thing we need to leave this room with?* | Creates shared focus. Prevents drift. |
| *What do you need from me today?* | Shifts from presenting to serving. Sets collaborative tone. |
| *Where are we stuck?* | Skips status updates. Goes straight to where value is added. |

### During discussion

| Question | What it does |
|----------|-------------|
| *Who haven't we heard from yet?* | Creates space for quieter voices. Models inclusive leadership. |
| *Are we solving the right problem?* | Recalibrates when discussion drifts into solution mode too early. |
| *What are we assuming here?* | Surfaces blind spots and groupthink. |
| *What would we do if we had half the time?* | Forces prioritization and reveals what's truly essential. |

### In 1-on-1s

| Question | What it does |
|----------|-------------|
| *What's energizing you right now?* | Starts positive. Reveals what motivates this person. |
| *What's draining you right now?* | Paired with the above: shows the full picture without leading. |
| *What does support from me look like for you?* | Prevents assuming you know what they need. |
| *Is there something we keep avoiding?* | Opens the door to difficult topics with shared ownership. |
| *What would you do differently if it were entirely your call?* | Reveals hidden initiative, ownership, and trust levels. |

---

## 5. Community and Governance

For board discussions, community calls, governance decisions, and the delicate art of leading through influence rather than authority.

| Question | What it does |
|----------|-------------|
| *What would the community say about this decision?* | Shifts from internal logic to external impact. |
| *What's the concern behind the objection?* | Moves past positional disagreement to underlying needs. |
| *Where do we agree? Let's start there.* | Finds common ground in polarized discussions. |
| *What would we need to see to change our mind?* | Introduces intellectual humility and testable criteria. |
| *Is this a decision or a discussion?* | Clarifies expectations. Prevents frustration on both sides. |
| *Who else should be in this conversation?* | Checks for missing perspectives before committing. |

---

## 6. Navigating Conflict and Tension

When the energy shifts, when someone pushes back, or when something feels unresolved. These are questions for moments that require courage and care.

| Question | What it does |
|----------|-------------|
| *Help me understand where you're coming from.* | Non-confrontational. Opens dialogue when positions harden. |
| *What would need to happen for this to work for both of us?* | Shifts from win/lose to shared problem-solving. |
| *What's really at stake for you here?* | Goes beneath the position to the interest. |
| *I sense there's more here. Am I reading that right?* | Gently names the elephant. Gives permission to be honest. |
| *What would it take to rebuild trust here?* | Forward-looking. Moves from blame to repair. |
| *Can we pause and check: are we still on the same side?* | Resets the dynamic when conversation turns adversarial. |

---

## 7. Self-Check: Before and After

Questions to ask yourself. These support the behavioral pattern observation practice. Use them as a brief ritual around meetings to build awareness over time.

### Before the conversation

| Question | Purpose |
|----------|---------|
| *What am I trying to achieve here?* | Clarifies your intention before you walk in. |
| *What does the other person need from this?* | Shifts perspective. Primes you for listening. |
| *Where am I likely to jump in too quickly?* | Pre-awareness of your pattern. Prepares the pause. |

### After the conversation

| Question | Purpose |
|----------|---------|
| *Where did I listen well?* | Reinforces positive patterns. |
| *Where did I fill the silence?* | Honest check without judgment. Data for the log. |
| *What did I learn that surprised me?* | Captures insight. Rewards curiosity. |
| *What question do I wish I had asked?* | Builds your repertoire for next time. |

---

### A final thought

The best question in any conversation is usually the second one. The first gets the topic on the table. The second, asked with genuine curiosity after you have really listened, is where the conversation actually begins.

*Ask less. Listen longer. Let the silence work.*
